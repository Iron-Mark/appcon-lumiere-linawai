# 04 — Winning patterns (what transfers to a 3-day virtual AI hackathon)

This file merges (a) what AppCon's own history rewards ([02](02-past-winners-analysis.md)), (b) the 2026 rubric ([03](03-judging-criteria-decoded.md)), and (c) general hackathon evidence (see §4 and [09-sources.md](09-sources.md)). Each pattern is stated as a rule you can act on in 72 hours.

## 1. Patterns from AppCon history that still apply

1. **Pick a mainstream Filipino pain that judges feel personally.** Every Grand Winner attacked traffic, jobs, road deaths, or plastic waste ([winners page](https://appcon.otismanila.com/winners)). In 2026 the sub-theme is drawn for you, but *within* it choose the most universally recognizable pain, not the cleverest niche.
2. **Build a closed loop, not a screen.** Winners sense → decide → notify/act (TRAVIS detects → dispatches; TransiTech predicts → tells drivers/passengers). A demo that ends in a dashboard is weaker than one that ends in an action reaching a person (SMS, push, printed slip, a barangay queue).
3. **Multi-stakeholder is a feature.** Passenger + driver + admin; donor + hospital; citizen + LGU. It signals systems thinking and lets you show Impact from two angles. Keep it to two roles in 3 days.
4. **Make the AI specific and measurable.** Prediction, detection, matching, routing, verification — with a number attached (accuracy on N cases, minutes saved). No Grand Winner was "a chatbot".
5. **Design an incentive or behavior mechanism.** ReVendo's points-for-bottles and SnapTrash's report-to-map are what made them memorable ([winners page](https://appcon.otismanila.com/winners)). Ask: what makes the user come back tomorrow?
6. **Frame for institutional adoption.** Champions talk about LGUs, DOTr, LTFRB, barangay health stations ([Corner Magazine](https://www.cornermagazineph.com/2025/07/05/grand-winner-of-appcon-2024-competition-transitech-smart-transportation-management-system-project-wins-top-prize/)). Name the unit that would run it and what it costs them.
7. **Ground truth beats mock data.** The 2024 champions collected real LRT data ([LinkedIn, Zuñiga](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jonhzuniga_appcon2024-cybergo-transitech-activity-7345677106397237249-ely7)). In 72 hours: use real open datasets (PSA, PAGASA, DOH, DepEd, LTFRB, data.gov.ph), scrape a real public source, or collect 30 real samples from your own team and friends.
8. **Experience with the organizer compounds.** The 2024 team leader was a 2023 awardee ([Corner Magazine](https://www.cornermagazineph.com/2025/07/05/grand-winner-of-appcon-2024-competition-transitech-smart-transportation-management-system-project-wins-top-prize/)). If a teammate has AppCon history, make them the pitch lead or the judge-liaison.
9. **Say "impact potential" and mean it.** The one recorded piece of OTis feedback praised the 2024 winner's "global impact potential" ([LinkedIn, Neypes](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jlrnrph_hala-totoo-ba-panalo-tayo-maam-we-did-activity-7346357023657140224-uiQz)). Show the replication path: same office exists in 1,600+ LGUs; same pattern in other ASEAN cities; a one-line Japan angle if honest.
10. **The novel concept wins Creativity even when the AI is immature.** Most Creative went to snap-to-report litter mapping whose builders admit the AI "still has a long way to go" ([LinkedIn, Esguerra](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/adrian-esguerra-44a899189_snaptrash-appcon2024-mostcreativeaward-activity-7345405198971912192-5TqF)). Put creativity in the concept and the input channel; keep the golden path boring and working.

## 2. Patterns from AppCon history that do NOT transfer in 2026

| Old pattern | Why it fails now | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware/IoT prototypes (3 of 5 Grand Winners) | 3 days, virtual, BYOD; no way to show a device credibly | Software-only; if the sub-theme begs for sensors, simulate a sensor feed from a public dataset and say so honestly |
| Thesis-style breadth (10 modules) | Weekly-report format rewarded volume; hackathon rewards one flawless golden path | 1 primary flow + 1 supporting flow; everything else is a roadmap slide |
| Months of self-study of new stacks | No time | Use stacks the three devs already share; decide in the first 30 minutes |
| One-school cohesion and faculty mentors (advisers recur across winning editions) | Teams are strangers matched by the organizer; no advisers | Explicit roles, a written 1-page charter, a single decision-maker for scope; treat Speaker Sessions and any mentors as your adviser — ask them one specific question about your idea |
| Capstone/thesis reuse (the norm among past winners) | Lottery sub-theme; 72 hours | Reusable *skills and scaffolding*, not a reusable project; the idea bank in [05](05-candidate-ideas-ranked.md) |
| Long promotional video | Judged mostly on live pitch + repo | 60–90 s backup demo video, but optimize the live run |

## 3. Patterns specific to the 2026 rubric and format

1. **AI-core, or nothing.** "AI is required and must be the core technology" ([site](https://appcon.otismanila.com/about)). The test: remove the model — does the product still work? If yes, AI is a garnish and you will lose Relevance and Technical Innovation.
2. **Beyond the wrapper.** In 2026 every team can call an LLM. Differentiators judges can verify in code: retrieval over a real corpus, structured outputs with validation, multimodal inputs (camera/voice), an agentic multi-step workflow with tools, a classical model where appropriate (forecasting, classification), and an eval harness.
3. **Filipino-context AI.** Taglish/Bisaya inputs, SMS/USSD reach, low-bandwidth modes, prepaid-data-conscious design — most global-hackathon templates ignore these and judges here will notice them.
4. **Maintainability is 10 points nobody else will claim.** README + architecture + tests + cost model ([03 §4](03-judging-criteria-decoded.md)).
5. **Presentation is the only channel in a virtual event.** Camera on, one screen, rehearsed handoffs, a recorded backup ([07](07-pitch-and-demo-playbook.md)).
6. **Creativity is 20% and the brief says "get crazy".** The organizers' own words: "No limits, no hesitation. Let loose and turn bold concepts into reality using AI as your tool" ([FB 19 Jan 2026](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1APYbQVGnT/)); purpose: "truly imaginative and unconventional digital ideas" ([site](https://appcon.otismanila.com/about)). A safe CRUD app with a chatbot will lose 8–10 points here to a team with a bold mechanism — but boldness must sit on top of a working golden path, not replace it.
7. **The organizer's own AI products hint at taste.** OTis sells AI that auto-drafts paperwork for care facilities from interview logs, AI agents/mini-apps for business workflows, and LINE-based estimate tools, under the slogan "make your work easier" ([OTis works page](https://otis.company/works)). Practical, workflow-embedded AI for non-technical users will read as "real" to OTis judges.
8. **You are being recruited, not just judged.** OTis runs AppCon to find talent and offshore partners ([OTis 2019 release](https://otis.company/archives/915)); the code may be used "for marketing and sponsors" ([site](https://appcon.otismanila.com/about)). So: be punctual and structured (Japanese business norms), answer briefly with evidence, credit teammates by role, make the README marketing-ready (tagline, screenshots, 90-second video) — a showcase-able repo is also a hireable one.
9. **Use sponsor tools visibly if any are provided.** The organizers asked sponsors specifically for GenAI development services ([primer slide 10](https://www.canva.com/design/DAG-QGNyPF4/CBOk-pFxBwUieoTgo0rMtA/view)) and sell "a special award named after your company" to Gold/Diamond sponsors ([primer slide 13](https://www.canva.com/design/DAG-QGNyPF4/CBOk-pFxBwUieoTgo0rMtA/view)). If a sponsor's API, model, or coding tool is offered, using it well (and saying so on slide 6) is a cheap edge for any sponsor award and costs nothing on the main rubric.

## 4. Patterns from the hackathon literature and comparable events (cited)

A dedicated research pass covered 20+ comparable Philippine/APAC events and the "what wins" literature (judges' write-ups, organizer guides, peer-reviewed studies); the full dossier with 130+ sources is in the project store (`internal/comparable-hackathons-and-winning-literature.md`) and the ones used here are listed in [09 §E](09-sources.md). The findings that survive cross-checking against AppCon's own history:

**How judges actually score**
1. **Whether the demo ran is the single largest differentiator.** A judge of three 2026 hackathons: "A working demo, live … beats a more ambitious project shown as slides almost every time … a live demo removes doubt"; "One complete path through the product beats four half-built paths" ([Kurbaitaev, 2026](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)). JetBrains' judging-table notes agree: "A strong project with a confusing demo loses to a simpler project that the judges understand" ([JetBrains, 2026](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)).
2. **Narrow beats platform; pick the sentence a judge will repeat.** "I scored the narrow projects higher consistently, and so did every other judge … Pick the sentence you want a judge to repeat to another judge an hour later. Build the demo that earns that sentence. Cut everything that does not" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)).
3. **Say what it is in the first sentence — then tell the story.** Teams "that lost ground opened with context. Market size, a personal story, a problem statement that took forty seconds to arrive at the product" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)); every JetBrains judge still said "start with the problem" so judges "share your frustration" ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). Resolution used in [07](07-pitch-and-demo-playbook.md): a 6-second real input, a one-sentence identity, then the person.
4. **Show something working within ~90 seconds** (Jono Bacon's rule, [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)); lablab's video structure puts the live demo at 0:30–2:30 of a 4-minute pitch ([lablab guide](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)).
5. **Be direct about what is real vs. mocked and name what breaks first.** "Admitting a limitation raised scores in my sheets"; judges ask "What part did you build this weekend?" and "What breaks first at scale?" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)); "Being honest about it reads as confidence" ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)).
6. **Rehearse the opening thirty seconds and record a 60-second insurance capture** — "That is where the score is set … Wifi fails at most events I judged" ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l)).
7. **Where the literature and AppCon disagree.** Generic judges say elaborate UI, framework choice, and clean architecture "nobody sees" do not move scores ([Kurbaitaev](https://dev.to/kurbaitaev/what-judges-actually-score-notes-from-a-year-of-hackathon-judging-3p4l); [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)). AppCon **explicitly scores UI/UX (10) and Maintainability (10) and reviews source code** ([site](https://appcon.otismanila.com/about)), so here they do — but spend the UI budget on legibility and consistency, not decoration, and keep the deck minimal and demo-centric.

**AI-hackathon-specific (2025–2026)**
8. **"Not a wrapper" is now a literal rubric anchor.** lablab scores whether "the AI [is] meaningfully integrated (not just a chatbot wrapper)" and asks whether the solution "only became possible with this generation of AI models" ([lablab guide](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)); a Seoul 2026 rubric's Technical Depth scale starts at "Basic wrapper around existing tools" and rises to "Novel technical contribution" ([Seoul rubric](https://github.com/Two-Weeks-Team/seoul-26th-april-hack-judges/blob/refs/heads/main/docs/official-track-rubrics.md)).
9. **Ship evidence, not adjectives.** Every winner of the Philippines' AI for Good Hackathon 2026 "shipped a model — fine-tuned, evaluated, and deployed"; the agritech winner cited "91% accuracy and works fully offline", the flood winner "up to four hours" of lead time and a pilot in three barangays ([Impact Hub Manila](https://impacthub.ph/article-ai-for-good-2026.html)). Seoul's Effectiveness & Evidence line rewards a "quantified success rate; benchmarked against baselines" ([Seoul rubric](https://github.com/Two-Weeks-Team/seoul-26th-april-hack-judges/blob/refs/heads/main/docs/official-track-rubrics.md)).
10. **Guardrails and humans-in-the-loop are scored, and PH judges say so.** DICT Undersecretary Yvette Cabrera, chairing that jury: "the responsibility to keep humans in the loop" ([Impact Hub Manila](https://impacthub.ph/article-ai-for-good-2026.html)); the mental-health winner routes high-risk chats to the DOH hotline. The 2025 GRAILS handbook gives Ethical Consideration 25% ([GRAILS](https://grails.hr.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Competition-Handbook-2025-edition.pdf)); USAII 2026 requires a risk-and-mitigation statement showing "where human judgment remains" ([USAII](https://aihackathon.usaii.org/content/docs/usaii-global-ai-hackathon-2026-student-guide.pdf)).
11. **Real local data beats mock data.** A GenAIus 2025 (GDG UP Manila) top-5 team: "we used mock data … That hurt our chances, and we didn't make it to the Top 3" ([LinkedIn, Cimanes](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cimanesdev_last-may-my-team-placed-in-the-top-5-of-activity-7352671196867411968--q3w)); Alibaba Cloud PH 2025's 2nd place grounded outputs in "publicly available local data" ([LinkedIn, Silverio](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lance-jacob-silverio-a02662361_late-post-first-time-joining-a-hackathon-activity-7398625792135536640-s_-U)).
12. **Deployed beats localhost; commits are evidence.** "A working local demo that can't be accessed by judges scores as if it doesn't work"; "an empty repo with one final push raises red flags"; "Pivoting after hour 12 … is almost always fatal"; "chaining 5 LLM calls when 1 would do adds latency and failure points" ([lablab guide](https://lablab.ai/guide/how-to-win-an-ai-hackathon)). A 2026 judge starts with `git log --oneline` and found iterative histories correlated with better architecture ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/the-git-log-never-lies)); Devpost's Gemini 3 hackathon investigated an entry whose public history began with one ~12,000-line commit ([Devpost](https://gemini3.devpost.com/)). Since AppCon reviews source in the organizer repo, **commit small and often from hour 1**.
13. **Engineer for demo-time failure.** Retry with exponential backoff on 429s ([OpenAI Cookbook](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/how_to_handle_rate_limits)); reserve a reliability block for "caching/demo mode, errors, retries, timeouts" and an offline fallback ([Since AI 72h](https://sinceai.ai/blog/how-to-build-a-demo-in-72-hours)).

**Team, continuation, and format research**
14. **Sustainability is judged on skill diversity + intent, not effort.** Long-term continuation of hackathon projects "is predicated on skill diversity among team members, their technical capabilities … and their intention to expand the reach of a project", while intensive short-term activity predicts *lower* long-term continuation ([Nolte, Chounta & Herbsleb 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3415216)). For Maintainability & Sustainability (10): a stranger-runnable README, a deploy URL, a named owner and a named next-user pilot.
15. **Team size helps creativity; divergent interests hurt; smaller events help.** Across 10,363 Devpost projects, number of team members had a positive effect on creative outcomes and "Different Interests" a significant negative one; larger events produced less creative projects ([Falk et al., CHI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713447); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04290)). AppCon's 5-person teams at a 100-person event sit in the favourable band — **if** the first hour converges the strangers on one shared interest inside the drawn sub-theme.
16. **Reuse is the norm; spend new-code budget on the differentiator.** Only ~9% of code in hackathon repos is written during the event ([Imam Mahmoud et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-022-10201-x)) — scaffold everything except the AI orchestration and evals.
17. **Virtual-event mechanics.** Integration freeze ~6 hours and demo recording ~3 hours before the deadline; one voice channel, one text channel, one shared doc; 10-minute syncs every ~4 hours ([Reskilll online guide](https://reskilll.com/blogs/online-hackathon-tips-2026-how-to-win-not-same-room/)); every live team should have "a short backup video" and rehearse the switch to it ([Rise In](https://www.risein.com/guide/hackathon-demo-day)).
18. **Familiar tools, prepared boilerplates.** "Use tools that you're already familiar with … Stacking a new framework, a new teammate, and a new problem domain all at once is a lot" ([JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)); Devpost winners pre-build "small boilerplates" ([Devpost](https://info.devpost.com/blog/tips-from-hackathon-winners)).

**What Philippine AI winners looked like in 2025–2026 (for idea selection)**

Filipino-language, on-device/offline, low-end-Android designs (Kumusta, SaktoFarm — [Impact Hub Manila](https://impacthub.ph/article-ai-for-good-2026.html)); reuse of existing infrastructure such as CCTV (TrashScan, PSC X People's Choice — [LinkedIn, FEU Tech](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/feu-tech-innovation-center_createinnovatecollaborate-technologydrivenbyinnovation-activity-7405940511355723776-rqh6)); document understanding for consumer protection (PaperProof contracts — [12in12](https://12in12.pro/projects/paperproof); Fee-ver medical bills — [LinkedIn, AUF](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beth-timbol-cuison-1b599a34b_aufccs-ccsdragons-bscs-activity-7398946324622159872-Oac9)); senior access to government services (Alalay.ph, same source); clinical decision support from cheap signals (DiaSight — [WVSU](https://wvsu.edu.ph/wvsu-devcomsoc-shines-light-on-eco-anxietis-among-youth-calls-for-climate-action-copy/)); agentic AI that acts for MSMEs (Caya, Byte Forward 2025 — [BusinessWorld](https://bworldonline.com/sparkup/2025/11/03/709568/filipino-students-showcase-tech-driven-business-solutions-at-51st-philippine-business-conference-and-expo/)).

## 5. The composite profile of a 2026 AppCon Grand Winner (synthesis)

- A **software-only AI system** for a **mainstream Filipino social pain inside the drawn sub-theme**, with **two user roles** and a **closed loop** ending in an action that reaches a person.
- **One flawless golden path**, live, on a deployed URL, plus a 60–90 s backup video.
- **Real data** (open dataset, scraped public source, or 30+ collected samples) and a **small eval table**.
- A **bold mechanism or interface** (incentive design, voice/SMS-first, camera-first, agentic workflow) that makes it memorable and earns Creativity.
- **Filipino-context details** (Taglish, low bandwidth, prepaid data, barangay-level deployment) that show relevance beyond a template.
- A **repo that reads like a product** (README, diagram, tests, evals, cost model, privacy note).
- A **rehearsed pitch** with a named user, a number, a live demo, and an institutional adoption ask — delivered by the most fluent speaker, with a teammate driving the demo.
